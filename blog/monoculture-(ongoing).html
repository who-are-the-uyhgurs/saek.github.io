Monoculture (ongoing)

YouTube quietly killed the trending tab. There wasn't any big drama. No one really visited it anymore. But I think it matters because it wasn't just a feature. It was the last thing that we all saw together. Everyone. Its death says something about what the internet has become. And I'm not sure how I feel about it because the system profits when you are alone.

This is not a conspiracy. This is just how sales and economics work. When you're watching something alone, you're easier to track, categorize, and sell to. Specific people watching specific content in private means ideal ad targeting. And you might have noticed that being alone or feeling lonely makes you vulnerable. You crave connection. And subconsciously, you might be wondering what's wrong with you or how to make this better. And it's not that easy. When you're online, the system fills that void with consumption. Buy this thing. Learn this thing. Wear this thing. Fix yourself somehow. Fix your skin. Fix your muscles. And the more isolated you feel, the more monetizable you become.

The internet used to be a lot less of a time sink for good and bad. It just wasn't as good at showing you exactly your interests. There used to be a bit of a monoculture, a mainstream, shared songs, shared jokes, shared videos that almost everyone who was online knew about. Gangnam Style or Friday or Double Rainbow. Everyone saw them. And if you haven't heard of these, you're young and that's okay. And I don't think those were just memes. They were points of contact. But nowadays, no one can agree on what actually is popular. The feed is hyperpersonalized and the culture is splintered. The loss of monoculture means a loss of shared language and even a loss of shared values. Like, what do you know that you have in common with someone else who is online or that you meet in real life? There's not that much anymore.

When it comes to the old YouTube page (and I don't want this to become some weird nostalgia thing) but personalization kind of killed discovery of new things for me. The old YouTube homepage had friction in a whole other way. You would just stumble into something weird and random that you wouldn't necessarily be served nowadays because it wasn't what you would look up or necessarily even click on, but it was there. It was served to you because no one had already predicted that no, you're not going to like that, you're going to click something else instead. So therefore, we never show you the weird video. And the interesting thing with that friction, even if sometimes it led to you just leaving the website because it didn't look that interesting to you, when you actually clicked it, it would expand who you were as a person.

But now that type of friction is seen as an inefficiency. You're shown what you're most likely to click again, again, and again. And that personalization feels comfortable, but it slowly closes the walls. And now it feels like we've totally lost the sense of friction. And it's led to us even avoiding friction in our real lives, which is part of why a lot of people seem to be talking more to chatbots than real people nowadays. Because friction was what used to make us brush up against people who weren't like us. It would make conversations harder because you didn't agree on everything, but it was also more human and you kind of felt a little bit closer to someone you wouldn't be close with usually after having that type of friction with them.

And now without that sort of friction, it's like we live in these smooth tunnels that are really slippery and fast and nice and increasingly alone, increasingly fragmented. Like, they (there) might be someone who is very similar to me but that just has a few different interests, and because of that they are 1,000 videos deep into that interest and I might be 1,000 videos deep into another interest and we just have nowhere to meet each other. Does that make sense?

Because algorithms don't only reflect our behavior, they shape it as well. It's a feedback loop. You can do one accidental search and it can shift your feed for like a week if you're unlucky. Overall, this system just makes you predictable and you become part of a cluster. You're profitable because they can put you in a cluster with a bunch of other people that will like similar things to you. So while they might have you pinned down a little bit better, they're also flattening you together with a bunch of people who are also like that, if that makes sense.

It's weird. It sounds like an oxymoron, but it's not, let me tell you. Because you don't really grow. We're not really checking what areas you can grow into with a little bit of friction. You're getting sorted into a smooth system that keeps you on the site. In a way, you're not being understood. You're being averaged into who the system wants you to be. Because you're not only watching a video and deciding, is this for me? Over time, what's being recommended to you will change you and your brain. And we had a long time of kind of meeting each other more and more with the internet. But the way I feel like the information systems are working now is that they're actually taking us further apart from each other into all these little echo chambers.

Compare that to your parents or whatever. They were watching, I don't know, the Beatles on the TV and like one or two TV channels. If you got to school the day after, everyone would know that the Beatles were on because that's what an extreme monoculture would be like.

But I think from context it's pretty clear what I mean with monoculture.

And if you (the non-existent you, who would I want to read this?) also felt like your feeds or your algorithms are just a little bit too personal to share. YouTube felt more social back in the day. You would share videos with each other. But now that the algorithms have taken over, my homepage feels like a secret. It says too much about me. You wouldn't really show your homepage to someone. You wouldn't show your algorithm necessarily. It's too revealing. I don't think I have anything that I'm hiding there necessarily, but they cater it to specifically to a person.

It's the irony of the internet. We're all more connected than ever in a way, but nothing feels shareable anymore. I don't want to share anything I find on the internet with someone. And that's why it's so easy to show me ads. They know that the people who watch this super deep nerdy stuff, like we are a specific breed.

Sure, articles and things can be an interesting topic for discussion with the right people and the right articles, but that can be applied with anything, like how the right type of person would find a unique boardgame fun given the right friends and circumstances.

You know, another thing I've noticed talking about algorithms is that for me, I try to understand whenever someone is angry. And something on Youtube that was a problem for me for a while was that, you know, I'm a man. Shocker. I'm sorry to say I'm a man. And there's a lot of systemic problems that are, you know, down to men having been in charge and stuff for so many, forever basically, right? But as a person living in modern society, sometimes it can feel like all that is on my shoulders a little bit.

So when I used to get on Youtube and I would watch someone, you know, school me on some kind of social justice thing. And I will listen because I want to learn and stuff. I used to like political science. I used to read feminism articles and period health logs (just for having the knowledge to care for my future wife), all that type of stuff. I'm an ally, you know, not to brag, whatever. But I will try to watch people who say kind of mean stuff or degrading stuff about men. Like I used to try to watch those videos on Youtube, the “men are trash” type videos, and I want to see like, oh, do they have a point? I want to understand it.

If people kind of just hate me for who I am or like hate the group of men for who they are, I kind of want to understand why. And to be honest, most of the time the people who really go hard on the whole “men are trash” type thing, they are not the best. Like, they don't make that much sense most of the time. You know, critique patriarchy and men in power and all that stuff, but the whole flattening of the group of men often gets poorly used. But I would watch it to be like, “Okay, do they have a good argument here?” And often they would not. And then I'm like, “Okay, whatever. I'll just continue.”

But then the algorithm knows that I kind of stopped on that video. So over time, because I was like, you know, I'll give them a shot, whatever. Because I think it's important to understand different viewpoints. I started getting more and more stuff, just like kind of man-hating stuff, and it got pretty useless and cluttered. Just going on Youtube made me pretty bored and felt like getting assaulted for something I had no awareness of and no part in. You know what I mean? It was pretty intense to get yelled at for something I never did in the first place.

It is interesting because the system only sees my attention, not my intention. It doesn't see what I'm looking for in that content. I guess it just thinks that I love it, that I'm a masochist or something. So my curiosity there was turned against me, and I'm sure it's confusing my algorithm very much. Or maybe they don't care. Maybe it'll keep me watching forever.

Anyway, when it comes to what's universal, I sometimes feel like the very last thing we have that we can all agree on is that money is pretty cool. And honestly, I kind of hate it. We don't share culture anymore. We don't really share stories in a broad way. What we do share is monetization. I think that's what everyone can see in every genre of YouTube: shameless monetization. Everyone is optimizing branding and selling in videos that also have ads on them. Even authenticity is packaged and sold back to us. At the end of the day, I don't think the algorithms or the internet care what we believe or what we really do. It mainly wants us to stay and spend.

I know YouTube has this satisfaction survey thing it runs when it tries to figure out, you know, did this make you feel better or worse? I've gotten it a handful of times over many, many years of watching. Honestly, I have such a hard time thinking that those surveys are going to make a meaningful difference. Maybe a little bit. I really feel like I want to control my experience way more.

Why can't I just search for stuff that is interesting in the way that I think is interesting? Like, okay, I watched a video and it was a good video and like, find me more stuff that is funny in the same way. Use the same type of information or style of delivering it or whatever. I can't be specific like that. It's just like, no, I'm going to get—like, for a different but just for context example, it's like if a funny person talks about vacuum cleaners, I'm going to get more content about vacuum cleaners. You know what I mean? Most of the time, because that's a nuanced thing. When it comes to nuanced stuff, it feels like things are not being picked up.

I was searching for some product the other day that sounded like a podcast host's name, and it wasn't a very popular product. So I found like two videos, like 100 views. Okay, no one's really using this, whatever. But then after that, I started getting just a bunch of recommendations of that podcast host's name. Or like that guy's podcast. It was honestly so stupid.

But yeah, YouTube didn't kill the trending tab. Really, the culture did. I think the system works exactly as it's designed. And there are a lot of upsides, like for example minorities that couldn't find content that really catered to them. I'm really happy they can find it. I don't want everything to be like, you know, a white guy doing funny bits or whatever. We've had that. But there's just so little to come together on. So little, even just in terms of ideals. Again, like with money, like commercialism and commodification of stuff—that's what we have. That's what we can all agree on as a broad culture, billions of people.

But you know, basic moral values or whatever? We don't have anything. It feels like it's just grift. And yeah, it pulls us apart because that's more profitable. You alone in your room on your phone or watching TV alone or whatever is more profitable than you watching with a bunch of people because then they can't show you individual ads. It's not worth it. We're watching alone because that's when we're most valuable.
